## Shared visitor guidance

At religious sites, cover shoulders and knees, lower your voice, and follow local instructions about shoes and photography. Ask before photographing people. Change out of swimwear before entering town or sacred spaces. Keep disagreements calm. [E1; annual review]

Editorial practical checklist: carry water, sun protection, a rain layer and footwear with grip. Obey beach flags, lifeguards and road closures. Do not enter the water when conditions or local instructions make it unsafe. Confirm transport pickup and the return journey before leaving town. Do not touch, feed or pursue wildlife. Agree the unit, weight, preparation charge and total before ordering seafood. These are precautionary recommendations, not claims about a particular business.

## Da Nang

### City overview

A coastal city suited to combining beach time, Cham art, sacred caves and short scenic outings. The Han River provides a useful city-centre reference, while the beach belt and Son Tra Peninsula shape the eastern side. Choose a few areas rather than treating every attraction as a walk from one hotel. [D1, D3, D4, D6]

### Orientation

- Central river area: use Han Market and the Museum of Cham Sculpture as reference points for food, culture and river walks. [D4, D6]
- Beach side: My Khe and the coast south of Son Tra suit early coastal walks; choose lodging here if beach access is your priority. [D1; lodging advice is editorial]
- Northeast: Son Tra and Linh Ung at Bai But are a separate scenic outing. [D3]
- Southeast: Marble Mountains lie about 8 km from the centre, in the direction of the coastal route toward Hoi An. [D2]
- Northward excursion: Hai Van Pass is the coastal mountain-road outing between Da Nang and Hue; it is not a central-city stop. [D1]

### Arrival and movement

Da Nang International Airport serves the city; rail, bus, taxi and ride-hailing options are also available. Compare the pickup location and complete fare before committing. Use a car with a driver for mountain outings if you are not comfortable riding; the pass is not a beginner riding lesson. Exact fares, timetables and journey times are not verified here. [D1; transport judgment is editorial; refresh schedules before travel]

### Seasonal planning

March–May is a useful starting window for planning. Do not interpret a seasonal guide as a promise of beach weather. Official pages are inconsistent about late-year weather: the city destination page praises September–October, while the national climate guide warns about central-coast storms from September and wet conditions around nearby Hoi An. Loka should avoid calling September–October reliably dry. Keep late-year outdoor plans flexible and check forecasts and warnings immediately before travel. [D1, W1; conflict flagged; review annually and before dated recommendations]

### Food and culture

Try mi Quang noodles and banh trang cuon thit heo, rice-paper rolls with pork and fresh vegetables. Mam nem is a fermented-fish dipping sauce; ask about ingredients rather than assuming a vegetable-heavy dish is vegetarian. Han Market introduces local foods and everyday trading alongside visitor shopping. [D1, F1, D6; annual review]

### Common planning mistakes

- Confusing the Linh Ung pagoda on Son Tra with similarly named pagodas elsewhere. Use “Bai But, Son Tra” when arranging a ride. [D3]
- Treating a lift ticket at Marble Mountains as full step-free access. Ask about the exact route and remaining stairs before buying. [D2; accessibility precaution is editorial]
- Relying on old show schedules or old US-dollar ticket estimates. Use the current official notice and local-currency price. [D5, D4]
- Packing the beach, Son Tra, Marble Mountains and Hai Van into one day. Leave space for transport, heat and weather. [editorial]

### Sample day routes

1. City and river: Museum of Cham Sculpture → lunch → Han Market → rest → Han River walk and Dragon Bridge. Add the performance only after confirming that evening's schedule. Editorial estimate: a relaxed full day with breaks; visits total roughly 3–4 hours, excluding meals and transport.
2. Coast and temple: an early My Khe walk → Linh Ung at Bai But → lunch and an unplanned afternoon. Editorial estimate: half to three-quarter day. Do not extend into restricted peninsula roads without checking access.
3. Caves and coastline: Marble Mountains in the cooler part of the day → lunch → beach or indoor rest. Editorial estimate: half day to a relaxed day. Hai Van merits a separate weather-dependent outing.

#### DN02 — Marble Mountains / Ngu Hanh Son
Description: Limestone and marble hills with caves and pagodas; Thuy Son is the main visitor focus. Why visit: combine sacred architecture, rock interiors and elevated views. Location/access: about 8 km southeast of central Da Nang; ticket gates and a separately charged lift serve Thuy Son. Visitor tip: plan for uneven steps and cave surfaces; ask which attractions each ticket covers. Editorial visit estimate: 2–3 hours. Official page lists adult Thuy Son entry at VND 40,000, Am Phu Cave at VND 20,000, and the lift at VND 15,000 each way. These are website-listed prices checked on the research date, not a future price guarantee. Opening hours and reservation requirement: not confirmed from current evidence. Sources: D2. Refresh: fees/hours before publication and monthly thereafter; same-day closures before a visit.

#### DN03 — Son Tra Peninsula and Linh Ung, Bai But
Description: Forested peninsula and a Buddhist pagoda at Bai But, around 10 km northeast of central Da Nang. Why visit: coastal views and a sacred-site visit without needing a long intercity trip. Location/access: arrange a return road transfer specifically to Linh Ung at Bai But. Visitor tip: obey local vehicle restrictions and do not assume every peninsula road is open; wildlife sightings are never guaranteed. Editorial visit estimate: 2–4 hours including a limited coastal outing; 45–75 minutes for the pagoda alone. Current hours, fees and road-access rules: not verified; check locally. Sources: D3. Refresh: access restrictions before every route recommendation; basic copy annually.

#### DN04 — Museum of Cham Sculpture
Description: A museum of Cham sculptural heritage, including sandstone and terracotta works. Why visit: see religious and architectural art in a focused museum setting. Location/access: 02, 2/9 Street; pair with the river area. Visitor tip: read current photography rules. Editorial visit estimate: 1–2 hours. Hours/fees/booking: unknown for current travel; the official tourism article is dated 2016, so its split hours and approximate dollar price are withheld. Sources: D4. Refresh: obtain current museum notice before publishing operations. #### DN05 — Dragon Bridge
Description: A dragon-shaped bridge over the Han River with scheduled fire-and-water performances. Why visit: see a recognisable piece of the city's riverfront architecture. Location/access: central Han River crossing; approach on foot from a lawful viewing area. Visitor tip: respect traffic control, anticipate water spray, and choose a pickup away from the busiest area. Editorial visit estimate: 30–60 minutes around the bridge, with additional waiting for a show. Official schedule page dated 2025 lists 21:00 Friday, Saturday and Sunday and major holidays. Holiday dates, cancellation notices, current access and any viewing-service charges need separate checks. Sources: D5. Refresh: schedule weekly, holiday and disruption notices before the evening.

#### DN06 — Han Market
Description: A central market selling food, produce, clothing and other goods. Why visit: browse local products and see a working trading space. Location/access: 119 Tran Phu Street; combine with a central river walk. Visitor tip: ask prices clearly, keep aisles clear and check destination import restrictions before buying foods to take home. Editorial visit estimate: 45–90 minutes. Current opening hours, admission policy and individual stall schedules: not verified; the dated official page is not used as a current tariff. Sources: D6. Refresh: operating details quarterly; food-import rules only from the destination authority when needed.

#### DN07 — Hai Van Pass
Description: A mountain road overlooking the coast between Da Nang and Hue. Why visit: the journey and viewpoints rather than a single building. Location/access: north of Da Nang; specify the scenic pass rather than assuming a transfer takes it. Visitor tip: postpone in poor visibility or unsafe weather; arrange a driver if riding competence is uncertain. Editorial visit estimate: 3–5 hours for a return excursion with stops, subject to the selected route. Road status, site hours and any ancillary fees: not verified. Sources: D1. Refresh: weather and road conditions on departure day.

## Nha Trang

### City overview and orientation

Nha Trang combines a city beachfront with religious landmarks, markets and marine-science exhibits. The central promenade is a useful base for short walks. Group north-side cultural visits separately from the Oceanography Museum at Cau Da to reduce backtracking. [N1, N2, N3; route grouping is editorial]

- Beachfront core: Tran Phu and the promenade provide a coastal reference for exploring the city. [N2]
- North-side culture: Po Nagar is north of the centre; pair it with a market visit rather than assuming it is next door to the southern museum. [N2; pairing is editorial]
- Inland city: Long Son sits at the foot of Trai Thuy hill, while the cathedral is on Thai Nguyen Street. [N2, N4]
- Southern museum outing: the Oceanography Museum lists its address as 1 Cau Da. [N3]

### Arrival and movement

Cam Ranh International Airport is outside central Nha Trang, approximately 30 km south according to the national tourism page. Allow a separate airport transfer; do not assume arrival at a city-centre airport. Shuttles, taxis and ride-hailing are listed, and a railway station serves the centre. Exact fares, routes, journey times and departure frequencies remain unverified. [N1; refresh before travel]

### Seasonal planning

January–August is generally the sunnier planning window; September–December is typically wetter. July and August can be hot. Keep beach and boat plans flexible: a favourable season does not guarantee calm seas, safe swimming or an operating excursion. Use land-based alternatives when conditions deteriorate. [W1; seasonal review annually, forecasts and marine access before travel]

### Food and culture

Look for bun ca sua, a noodle soup combining fish cake and jellyfish; nem nuong, grilled pork served with wrapping ingredients; and banh can, small rice-flour cakes cooked in individual moulds. Fillings and dipping sauces vary. Seafood may be sold by weight: confirm the unit and selected weight before preparation. Tell staff about dietary needs and ask about sauces as well as the main ingredient. [N5; annual review]

Religious landmarks remain places of worship. Give ceremonies space, dress modestly and ask before photographing worshippers. [N2, E1]

### Common planning mistakes

- Forgetting that the airport transfer is separate from city sightseeing. [N1]
- Assuming a beach destination is rain-free throughout the year. [W1]
- Treating an old island-tour description as proof that a particular protected area or dive site is open. Access, conservation restrictions, weather and vessel arrangements must be confirmed independently. [editorial precaution]
- Choosing seafood without confirming weight and total cost. [N5]
- Filling every daylight hour with outdoor stops; use the museum as a possible alternative after confirming it is open. [editorial]

### Sample day routes

1. Culture and city: Long Son → cathedral exterior or permitted visitor session → lunch → Dam Market → evening promenade. Editorial estimate: a full day with rests; attraction visits approximately 3–4 hours. If worship or access prevents a cathedral visit, omit it.
2. Heritage and sea: Po Nagar in a cooler part of the day → lunch → relaxed beachfront time. Editorial estimate: half day or an easy full day; swimming is conditional.
3. Marine-science morning: Oceanography Museum → lunch → free afternoon. Editorial estimate: half day including transport. This is a land-based alternative, not a guarantee of operation during severe weather.

### Destination cards

#### NT01 — Nha Trang Beach and Promenade
Description: The city's urban beach edge and walking promenade. Why visit: an easy coastal walk between sightseeing stops. Location/access: central beachfront along Tran Phu. Visitor tip: choose a named meeting point and follow swimming warnings. Editorial visit estimate: 1–2 hours. Current access, lifeguard schedule, equipment charges and swimming conditions: not verified. Sources: N1, N2. Refresh: beach advisories on visit day.

#### NT02 — Po Nagar Cham Towers
Description: Historic Cham towers north of the city, still associated with worship. Why visit: experience brick temple architecture and living religious heritage. Location/access: plan a road transfer from the centre. Visitor tip: give worshippers priority. Editorial visit estimate: 60–90 minutes. Current hours, fees and booking requirements: unknown; verify with the site. Sources: N2. Refresh: operations monthly; festival access before travel.

#### NT03 — Long Son Pagoda
Description: A Buddhist pagoda at Trai Thuy hill with a large white Buddha above it. Why visit: temple art and a hill setting. Location/access: inland Nha Trang; confirm mobility needs before the upper ascent. Visitor tip: do not assume the whole route is step-free. Editorial visit estimate: 60–90 minutes. Current hours, admission and booking requirements: unknown. Sources: N2. Refresh: operations quarterly; worship restrictions on the day.

#### NT04 — Dam Market
Description: A market for produce, household goods, clothing and souvenirs. Why visit: everyday trading and browsing. Location/access: city-market stop; use the local name Cho Dam for your ride. Visitor tip: compare prices politely and keep walkways clear. Editorial visit estimate: 45–75 minutes. Current building access, hours, fees and stall availability: unverified. Sources: N2. Refresh: market layout and operations before publication, then quarterly.

#### NT05 — Museum of Oceanography
Description: Marine exhibits, preserved specimens and aquariums. Why visit: learn about marine environments beyond a beach visit. Location/access: 1 Cau Da, Nha Trang; arrange transport to the museum's entrance. Visitor tip: follow exhibit photography instructions and allow for indoor and outdoor sections. Editorial visit estimate: 1.5–2.5 hours. Operator homepage lists 06:00–18:00 daily, checked 2026-10-17; no future guarantee. Current ticket price and booking requirement: not established in the checked evidence. Sources: N3. Refresh: hours/fees monthly and before a dated visit.

#### NT06 — Nha Trang Cathedral
Description: A Gothic-style Catholic cathedral on Thai Nguyen Street. Why visit: architecture and the city's religious heritage. Location/access: central city; use only the entrance staff make available. Visitor tip: sightseeing must give way to services; do not use an alternative entrance to bypass a closure. Editorial visit estimate: 30–60 minutes if visitor access is permitted. Current sightseeing hours, fees and reservations: unknown. The official tourism article dates to 2009 and cannot establish current entry arrangements. Sources: N4. Refresh: confirm directly before publication or a visit.
